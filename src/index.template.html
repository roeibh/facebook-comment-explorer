Expand and search every comment on a Facebook post

Facebook shows you ten comments at a time and hides replies behind “View 4 replies”. This bookmarklet loads all of them, then gives you a search box that actually works — plus thread filtering and CSV / JSON export.

How to use it

  1. Drag the button above onto your bookmarks bar.
  2. Open any Facebook post — a group post, a Page post, or someone’s profile post.
  3. Click the bookmark. It switches the sort to All comments, scrolls until Facebook stops sending more, and opens every “View N replies” it finds.
  4. Search, filter, jump to a comment on the page, or export the results.

In one live group-post test it loaded 443 comments in 40 seconds. A profile-post test loaded 303, with no missing authors or timestamps. Facebook can throttle repeated runs, so speed varies. The panel shows progress against the post’s own comment counter.

What it does

↻Loads everything

Switches to “All comments”, keeps the list scrolling, and clicks every reply expander until nothing new arrives.

⌕Search that works

"quoted phrase", -exclude and @author. Matches are highlighted, and any language works — including right-to-left text.

↳Whole threads

Replies are nested under the right parent, up to three levels deep. Click “4 replies” to read one conversation on its own.

↗Jump to the comment

Click any result and the real comment scrolls into view on the page and flashes, so you can reply or react to it.

⇩Export

Download the current filtered set as JSON or CSV, or copy it as plain text. Author, time, reactions, reply parent and a permalink for each comment.

⇅Sort

Page order, newest, oldest, most reactions, or longest — orderings Facebook does not offer you.

Inside the panel, / jumps to the search box and Esc steps back out of a thread or clears the search before closing the panel.

Privacy

Everything runs in your own browser, on the page you already have open and are already logged into. There is no server, no analytics, no account, and nothing is uploaded anywhere. The bookmarklet reads comments from the rendered page and builds a panel next to it — exports are generated locally and saved by your browser. The entire source is on GitHub and is short enough to read in one sitting.

Frequently asked questions

What is Facebook Comment Explorer?

Facebook Comment Explorer is a free, open-source bookmarklet that loads the comments and replies on a Facebook post into a searchable panel. It can filter whole threads, jump back to a comment on the post and export the current results as CSV or JSON.

Why can’t I just use Ctrl+F on a Facebook post?

Because the comments aren’t there yet. Your browser’s find function only searches text that has actually been rendered, and Facebook loads about ten comments at a time, keeps replies collapsed, and truncates long comments behind “See more”. Ctrl+F searches the handful you can see and misses the several hundred you can’t. Loading everything first is what makes find — and the search box in this tool — useful.

How do I expand all comments on a Facebook post?

Manually: set the comment sort to “All comments”, scroll to the bottom over and over until nothing more loads, then click every “View N replies” and every “See more” link. On a busy post that is several hundred clicks. This bookmarklet does exactly that sequence for you and then tells you how many it got.

Can I export Facebook comments to CSV or JSON?

Yes. The panel has JSON, CSV and “Copy text” buttons, and they export whatever is currently filtered — so you can search for a keyword and export only the matches. Each row has the comment id, author, level (top-level or reply), who it replied to, timestamp, reaction count, the text, and a permalink. The CSV is UTF-8 with a byte-order mark, so Hebrew, Arabic and accented characters open correctly in Excel.

Does it work on group posts, Page posts and profile posts?

All three. Facebook uses two different internal comment formats — group posts use numeric comment ids, while profile and Page posts use base64 ones and lay the links out differently — and the script handles both.

It stopped before loading every comment. Why?

Facebook paces how fast it will send comments, and throttles harder if you have already pulled a lot in a short window. When that happens the panel says so — “Loaded 84 of ~470” — rather than pretending it finished. Press the ↻ button to resume from where it stopped; it picks up rather than starting over. Waiting a minute between attempts helps.

Do I need to install an extension?

No. A bookmarklet is just a bookmark whose address happens to be JavaScript instead of a web address. It has no background process, no permissions, and no access to anything except the page you run it on, at the moment you click it. Nothing runs until you click.

Which browsers does it work in?

It has been tested in desktop Chrome. Other desktop Chromium browsers should work; Firefox and Safari remain unverified. Mobile browsers are unsupported because running a bookmarklet on facebook.com is impractical on iOS and Android.

Is this against Facebook’s rules? Can my account get banned?

It clicks buttons and scrolls, which is what you would do by hand; it does not use any private API, log in for you, or collect anything from people who are not already visible to you on that post. That said, Facebook’s terms discourage automated interaction, and rapid repeated use of any automation can get a session rate-limited. Use it on posts you can already read, at a human pace, and be sensible about what you do with other people’s comments — they may be personal data under GDPR or similar laws where you are.

How is this different from a Chrome extension for Facebook comments?

An extension asks for standing permission to read and change your data on the sites it covers, updates itself silently, and keeps running in the background. This is one bookmark containing code you can read, which does nothing at all until the moment you click it on a post you have open. The trade-off is that a bookmarklet cannot remember anything between runs.

Can I use it to count comments, or find who said something?

Yes to both. The header shows the total and the filtered count, and the panel breaks it down into top-level comments and replies. To find who said something, search the phrase; to see everything one person wrote, search @theirname.
